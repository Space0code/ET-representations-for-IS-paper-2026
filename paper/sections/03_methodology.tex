\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Data and Eye-Tracking Representations}

The data come from an ongoing multimodal study in which office workers are
recorded during ordinary work at their own workplace. The wider collection
comprises an RGB camera, a screen-mounted eye tracker, a microphone and
screen-activity logs; this paper uses the eye-tracking modality only. The
dataset is neither complete nor public, so this is a preliminary study on the
cohort available at the time of writing: 17 participants and 657 full-workday
recordings, between 19 and 79 recordings per participant (median 32). The
tracker samples at 60\,Hz, and we retain five channels: left and right pupil
size, gaze $x$ and $y$, and the average eye--screen distance.

Labels come from an experience-sampling protocol. Participants are prompted
several times per working day with a short questionnaire covering engagement,
affect, fatigue, stress, physical comfort, workplace interactions, sleep and
productivity. Our target is the absorption item, \enquote{Right now I am
immersed in my work}, one of three momentary engagement items answered at
every prompt and rated on a seven-point scale from 0 to 6; we call it the
\emph{engagement rating}. Each prompt labels the 15 minutes of recording that
precede it, and only those intervals enter this study.

Recordings are segmented into three-second windows with a 2.25-second step
(25\,\% overlap). Every window carries an identifier built from the
participant, the source recording and the position of the window inside that
recording; the four representation exports are joined on this identifier, so
all models see exactly the same windows. Windows with too little usable gaze
are discarded before embedding (at least 30\,\% valid frames and at least 32
valid frames), and remaining gaps inside a retained window are linearly
interpolated. Of roughly 7.3 million recorded windows, only those inside a
labelled interval are usable; 177{,}244 of them yield handcrafted features,
157{,}685 also pass the encoder quality control, and 140{,}531 carry a numeric
engagement rating. That final cohort---about 2\,\% of all recorded windows,
and between 2{,}136 and 25{,}447 windows per participant---is what we model.
Using labelled windows only discards a great deal of recorded data, but it
keeps a preliminary experiment tractable and the target unambiguous.

Display resolutions range from 1920$\times$1080 to 3840$\times$2160 pixels.
They differ between participants and, for a few participants, between that
person's own recordings, so gaze coordinates are normalised to $[0,1]$ using
the resolution declared in each source recording rather than one value per
participant.
Out-of-screen coordinates are retained for modelling; 94.7\,\% of samples fall
on the display, and only those enter the descriptive density map in
Figure~\ref{fig:descriptive}(a), which shows the expected central
concentration together with broad coverage of the screen.

Four representations are computed from these windows. \textbf{Raw} keeps the
five channels, truncates or pads each to 120 samples and flattens them into a
600-dimensional vector. \textbf{Handcrafted features} start from 199 exported
columns; 18 identifier, timestamp and questionnaire columns are removed
structurally so that no survey metadata can leak into the model, leaving 181
numeric candidates covering blink counts and durations, fixation and saccade
statistics, pupil size and velocity moments, eye--screen distance statistics
and per-channel missingness. \textbf{GazeMAE embeddings} come from the public
micro--macro autoencoder checkpoints applied to gaze position and velocity
resampled to 500\,Hz, with the 128-dimensional position and velocity codes
concatenated into 256 dimensions \cite{bautista2020gazemae}. \textbf{MOMENT
embeddings} come from the frozen \textsc{MOMENT-1-large} encoder applied to
the same five channels with an input length of 512 and mean pooling, giving
1{,}024 dimensions \cite{goswami2024moment}. Both encoders are used out of the
box; no weights are updated.

\subsection{Experimental Design}

Engagement ratings are strongly person-specific: the participant means span
2.2 to 4.9 on the 0--6 scale (Figure~\ref{fig:descriptive}(b)). An
absolute threshold would therefore mostly encode who the participant is rather
than when they were engaged, so we binarise relative to each person's own
level.
Inside every fold, the ratings of each training participant are mean-centred
separately, and the held-out participant's ratings are centred with the
unweighted mean of the training participants' means; a window is positive when
its centred rating exceeds zero. Over the cohort this gives 56.3\,\% positive
windows, ranging from 33\,\% to 72\,\% per participant.

The primary protocol is leave-one-subject-out cross-validation over the 17
participants. Everything data-dependent is fitted inside the fold:
handcrafted-feature filtering (dropping columns with more than 60\,\% missing
values, zero variance, or absolute Pearson correlation above 0.8 with a
retained column, which leaves 58--60 of the 181 candidates), median
imputation, and standardisation are all estimated on the training participants
and then applied unchanged to the held-out participant.

Three predictors are run on every representation: a most-frequent baseline, a
random forest (300 trees, balanced class weights) and an MLP
(1028--512--256--128--64 units, GELU activations, layer normalisation, Adam
with learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$ and weight decay $10^{-3}$, batch size
1024, at most 200 epochs with early stopping after 15 epochs without
improvement). A single seed (42) is used throughout. The
representation~$\times$~model grid is a robustness check on the representation
ranking rather than a model comparison.

We report accuracy as the primary metric, together with its improvement over
the baseline paired within folds, and balanced accuracy, macro-F1 and ROC-AUC
as secondary discrimination measures; all are aggregated as unweighted means
over participants. One participant rated every window below the training
centre, so that test fold contains a single class: accuracy is defined for all
17 folds and the remaining metrics for 16. As a contextual check we also run a
one-step persistence predictor within recordings, carrying the last observed
label forward in rolling blocks of 300 calibration and 90 test windows; it is
a different, within-participant protocol and is not comparable with the main
comparison.

\section{Discussion}

On this dataset, raw signals gave the highest observed scores among the four representations for binary engagement inference in unseen knowledge workers, with handcrafted features providing the strongest overall alternative and no improvement from the two frozen embeddings.
The small margins over chance show that three seconds of gaze does not make momentary self-reported engagement well predictable across people, whichever representation is used.

We interpret the lack of improvement from the frozen encoders as a mismatch between their pretraining data and this task.
GazeMAE was pretrained on controlled stimulus-viewing corpora and MOMENT on generic time-series benchmarks.
Neither was pretrained for day-long, spontaneous office gaze with its dropouts, idle stretches and heterogeneous displays.
These results concern frozen transfer only, and the next step is to fine-tune the encoders on office gaze, starting with GazeMAE, whose result depended on the classifier (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

For practical use, raw signals scored best here and offer the simplest input pipeline, requiring no feature engineering, pretrained checkpoint or encoder inference pass.
Handcrafted features should still be considered: they give the strongest overall alternative, they remain interpretable, and our earlier feature-based analyses found them effective on related eye-tracking targets \cite{bozak2024emotion,bozak2026workload}.
Our results do not support a frozen general-purpose encoder as the default choice.

Several limitations bound these conclusions: the study uses one unpublished and still-growing dataset with 17 participants.
The target is one noisy self-report assigned retrospectively to a 15-minute interval and binarised with a per-fold centring rule.
Overlapping windows and repeated labels within each interval reduce the effective sample size.
The results depend on one windowing scheme, one seed, one set of quality-control rules and two specific public checkpoints.
Future work should validate the comparison on further datasets, explore calibrated personalisation for new users, and test longer windows and aggregated targets that match the granularity at which engagement is actually reported.

\section{Conclusion}

We compared raw multichannel windows, handcrafted ocular features and frozen GazeMAE and MOMENT embeddings under one identical, leakage-safe leave-one-subject-out protocol for momentary engagement inference in knowledge workers.
Raw windows produced the highest observed result with 0.536 balanced accuracy and 0.551 ROC-AUC on left-out participants, and out of the box the frozen encoders did not improve on them, despite their higher preprocessing and compute cost.
For practitioners with limited labelled data, raw windows or handcrafted features are the better starting point, while frozen encoders need domain-specific validation or adaptation before use.

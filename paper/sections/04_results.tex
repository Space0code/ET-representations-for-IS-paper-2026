Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the main comparison, in which every representation--model combination sat close to chance.
The highest observed result was the raw signal with the MLP, at 0.536 balanced accuracy, 0.036 above the 0.500 chance level, and it was also highest on macro-F1 (0.468), ROC-AUC (0.551) and plain accuracy (0.538).
Handcrafted features followed, reaching 0.521 balanced accuracy with the MLP and improving on chance with both classifiers.
GazeMAE reached 0.509 balanced accuracy with the random forest and fell to 0.493, below chance, with the MLP\@.
MOMENT stayed at chance with both classifiers.

Handcrafted features ranked second on every metric except plain accuracy, where GazeMAE was second.
The balanced-accuracy margins over chance ranged from $-0.007$ to $+0.036$, smaller than the spread across held-out participants, whose balanced-accuracy standard deviations ranged from 0.010 to 0.063.
The MLP improved raw and handcrafted inputs but lowered both embedding results.
A flexible head may help when a representation already carries useful signal, but the effect is small, so this interpretation remains tentative and requires further statistical validation.

Figure~\ref{fig:tsne} shows the same difficulty geometrically: none of the four representations arranges windows by engagement rating, and the colours are as mixed in the learned embeddings as in the raw signal.
Colouring the identical projections by participant is more informative.
Raw signals form clear participant-dominated patches, handcrafted features and GazeMAE group more weakly, and the MOMENT projection is close to uniform.
The clearest visible structure is therefore person-specific, and the weaker grouping in the frozen embeddings may reflect smoothing by the encoders.
Because the projections are exploratory, this interpretation remains a conjecture.

As a check on the label series, a one-step persistence predictor was evaluated over 1{,}434 temporal blocks.
Participant-averaged accuracy reached 0.999, while balanced accuracy reached 0.960 and macro-F1 reached 0.965 over the 13 participants with two-class test blocks.
Each questionnaire answer is broadcast unchanged to every window of the preceding 15-minute interval (Section~\ref{sec:method}), so most consecutive window pairs share a label by construction.
Because the predictor uses observed labels within participants, its result cannot be compared directly with the left-out-participant results above.

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the main comparison. Every
representation--model combination sits close to the majority baseline. The
best result is the raw signal with the MLP, at 0.538 accuracy, 0.014 above the
paired baseline, and it is also best on balanced accuracy (0.536), macro-F1
(0.468) and ROC-AUC (0.551). Handcrafted features come next and improve on the
baseline with both classifiers (up to 0.531 accuracy and 0.535 AUC). The two
frozen encoders trail: GazeMAE reaches 0.535 accuracy with the random forest
but falls to 0.520, below the baseline, with the MLP, while MOMENT is at or
below the baseline on every metric and its AUC (0.504) is indistinguishable
from chance.

The ordering raw $\geq$ handcrafted $>$ GazeMAE $>$ MOMENT holds across all
four metrics, but the absolute differences are small---0.003 to 0.017 in
accuracy---and far smaller than the spread across held-out participants, whose
accuracy standard deviations range from 0.11 to 0.29. We therefore read the
ordering as a descriptive tendency in this cohort and attach no significance
claim to it. GazeMAE in particular warrants a flag rather than a verdict: it
is the only representation whose sign changes with the classifier, whereas raw
signals and handcrafted features improve on the baseline with both, so its
usefulness here is an open question rather than a settled negative result. A
related and tentative observation is that the MLP helps exactly those
representations that carry some signal and hurts the two embedding sets; one
possible reading is that a more flexible head pays off only when there is
something to fit, but the effect is small and we do not build on it.

Figure~\ref{fig:tsne} gives a geometric view of the same difficulty. In
two-dimensional t-SNE projections none of the four representations arranges
windows by engagement rating; the colours are mixed everywhere, in the learned
embeddings as much as in the raw signal. Colouring the identical projections
by participant instead reveals some local grouping for raw signals and
GazeMAE, suggesting that what structure these representations do capture is at
least partly person-specific rather than state-specific.

For context, a one-step persistence predictor applied within recordings
reaches 0.999 accuracy, 0.960 balanced accuracy and 0.965 macro-F1 over 1{,}434
temporal blocks. This is a sanity check on the label series rather than a
competitive result: because each questionnaire answer is broadcast unchanged
to every window of the preceding 15-minute interval (Section~\ref{sec:method}), most
consecutive window pairs share a label by construction. It is not comparable
with the unseen-participant results above.

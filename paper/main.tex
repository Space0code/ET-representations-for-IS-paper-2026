\begin{abstract}
	Eye tracking is an appealing sensor for inferring momentary psychological states at work.
	Before training a model, a practitioner must decide how to represent a gaze window.
	We compared four representation families on identical three-second windows drawn from knowledge workers across 657 full-workday recordings: raw multichannel signals (pupil size, gaze position and eye--screen distance), handcrafted ocular features, and embeddings obtained from pretrained eye-tracking (GazeMAE) and time-series (MOMENT) encoders, both used frozen.
	The target was a single self-reported item measuring momentary engagement.
	All four were evaluated under leave-one-subject-out cross-validation with per-fold preprocessing, using a random forest and an MLP classifier against a majority baseline.
	Raw signals gave the highest observed performance on the left-out participant (0.536 balanced accuracy), handcrafted features followed closely overall, and neither frozen encoder improved on the raw signal.
	Absolute discrimination was weak throughout (best ROC-AUC 0.551) and between-participant variability exceeded the differences between representations, so we report the ranking as preliminary, cost-aware guidance.
\end{abstract}
